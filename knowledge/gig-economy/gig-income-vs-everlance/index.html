GIG Income vs. Everlance for Gig Drivers

Everlance can automatically log individual drives. GIG Income lets you start one GPS session for a delivery shift and stop it when you finish.

VM LAB develops GIG Income. This comparison draws on Everlance’s published documentation and GIG Income’s current iPhone features.

How each app records your driving

What happens when you wait for the next DoorDash order?

Say you finish your first delivery, park, and wait five minutes for another order. If Everlance’s Auto-Detection treats that stop as the end of a drive, your next drive can show up as a separate trip. Over a shift you may end up with several entries to check and classify. Time spent parked and available for orders can fall between driving records, even though you still consider it part of your shift.

In GIG Income, tap Start before your first delivery and Stop when you finish working. If you leave the session running while waiting for orders, the wait stays in the recorded work time. Your logged mileage and work time stay together in one session. If you take a personal break, pause and resume when you return.

What about school drop-offs and other personal errands?

With automatic detection on, a grocery run or school drop-off can appear in the trip list. You can classify those drives as Personal and keep them out of work records. Everlance also offers classification settings if you want less cleanup.

With GIG Income, GPS tracking starts when you tap Start. If you stick to starting it for work and stopping when work ends, separate personal drives stay out of the log. If you forget to start, you can add the trip manually afterward.

Which workflow fits your shift?

Everlance’s Auto-Detection suits drivers who want their drives logged even when they forget to start tracking. Its manual mode lets you choose trip boundaries yourself. GIG Income suits drivers who want to explicitly record a shift’s miles and work time together with earnings and expenses.
